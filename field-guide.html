A program you can inspect.
A body you can understand.

A Quineling carries a finite program and a mathematical description of its body. The program calculates when you ask it to run. The renderer uses the body description to draw a pose. An operation owns a region you can select and inspect.

New here? Run the first water lesson. This guide explains why the bodies work and provides definitions to look up when using the tools. You can read the explanations or jump to a control or parameter.

First, ask what changes

Source is the complete authored program, including its body description. Editing its operations, constants, geometry, pigment or authored movement creates a new source identity. A body edit can preserve the calculation while changing the complete source.

Run inputs are observations supplied to declared input ports. In the typed workspace and task examples, changing them keeps the same source and body. In the older gallery, choosing a scenario instead writes its values into a new source. The creation workspace’s local grammar also places the data written in your thought into the program.

View controls choose a pose, a selected operation, an overlay or an existing record. They do not run a calculation. Local world controls advance Ranch social state or change participation. Ranch ticks, animation time and task execution are separate.

A quine is a program that emits its own complete source. Quinelings use a constructor that does this. Their wave and RGB genomes store recoverable source data. The picture is a visual index into that program; recovering source requires the data, not a screenshot.

Five ways to build a body

Current authoring chooses a construction from actual graph structure. A qualifying guard takes precedence, then branching, then effects, then convergence. Other pipelines use phyllotaxis. The complete selected description is retained in source.

Clifford flow

A two-coordinate recurrence traces a finite orbit. Occupancy and forward images of critical curves shape its visible tissue. Qualifying guards select it.

x′ = sin(a y) + c cos(a x)
y′ = sin(b x) + d cos(b y)

Quadratic Julia

Repeated squaring of a complex number makes branching boundaries at several scales. Fanout of at least two selects this construction when no qualifying guard takes precedence.

z′ = z² + c

Toroidal weave

A small winding wraps around a larger ring. An effect operation selects it when the earlier rules do not apply.

Logarithmic mantle

Rolled ribbons follow an expanding spiral. Convergence of at least two inputs selects it after the earlier rules.

r = 0.1 exp(growth × θ)

Phyllotaxis fan

Tapered, folded petals turn by the golden angle. This is the fallback for other pipelines.

golden angle = π(3 − √5)

“Guard” here follows the authoring classifier: it recognizes named guard/filter/if/condition operations and certain computed action guards. A comparison alone does not necessarily select Clifford. Renaming a program does not select its portrait.

These are bounded numerical constructions. Julia is a finite approximation of a fractal boundary. A positive finite expansion estimate for a Clifford orbit is evidence about that tested sample, not proof of chaos. More samples can reveal detail without establishing an infinite mathematical result.

A small mathematical vocabulary

Parameter
A named number or choice used by a formula. “Spread” can widen a ribbon; “iterations” sets how often a recurrence is evaluated. Parameters have different meanings in different constructions.
Coordinate, axis and scale
A coordinate locates a point. Here x usually runs across the picture, y vertically and z in depth. Multiplying an axis by a scale changes its size along that direction.
Radians, π and 2π
Radians measure angles. π radians is half a turn; 2π is a full turn. A phase moving from 0 to 2π traverses one authored cycle.
Sine and cosine: sin and cos
Smooth repeating functions bounded between −1 and 1. Together, (cos θ, sin θ) traces a unit circle. Multiplying a sine by an amplitude sets how far it moves; multiplying its input by a frequency changes how many oscillations fit.
Phase, frequency, amplitude and lag
Phase says where you are in a cycle. Frequency controls repetition through space or time. Amplitude controls movement size. Lag delays one part relative to another. A spatial frequency is not necessarily an animation speed.
Harmonic and quasiperiodic
A harmonic repeats at a whole-number multiple of a base frequency. Combining an irrational frequency such as √2 with the base generally removes a common finite repeat period when both contributions remain active.
Exponential and logarithmic spiral
exp(x) means e raised to x, where e is about 2.718. In a logarithmic spiral the radius grows by a repeated factor as the angle advances, rather than adding the same distance each turn.
Complex number and recurrence
A complex number has two parts, written x + iy, with i² = −1. You can picture it as a point (x,y). A recurrence repeatedly feeds its result back into the same rule. Julia’s rule squares that number and adds the fixed number c.
Escape time
How many recurrence steps a sampled point takes to exceed a chosen radius, within the allowed iteration budget. A point that does not escape within that budget is not thereby proved to stay bounded forever.
Golden angle and phyllotaxis
The golden angle is about 137.5 degrees. Turning each successive petal by this angle spreads them around the center without lining them up at a short repeating angular interval. Phyllotaxis names this kind of arrangement.
Torus, major radius and minor radius
A torus is a ring-shaped surface. Its major radius goes around the large ring; its minor radius describes the smaller cross-section wrapping around it.
Chart, u, v and component
A chart is a patch described by two coordinates. Woven ribbons use u from 0 to 1 along their length and v from −1 to 1 across them. A component selects a particular ribbon or part. Assembly charts use their own documented coordinates.
Territory and owner
A source-declared patch belongs to an operation. Selecting the operation highlights its patch. Ownership helps inspect the program; it does not mean the calculation physically happens at that point.
Compression, Jacobian and contour
A Jacobian describes how a small coordinate change stretches or compresses a mapped patch. Projected compression can make a fold brighter. A contour follows a chosen level or condition in a sampled field. These material rules approximate a visual effect; they are not a complete optical simulation.
Grid, budget and resolution
A grid lays out sample locations. A budget limits computation or the number of drawn samples. More resolution can cost more time. Neither is a promise that every continuous feature has been captured.
Depth, fanout and convergence
Graph depth is the longest dependency chain. Fanout counts outgoing input connections; convergence counts incoming ones. These are graph features, distinct from spatial depth in the drawing.
Deterministic
The same admitted source, inputs and relevant state produce the same result. The same authored body and phase produce the same sampled pose.
Canonical source, identity and genome
Canonical source is the program’s consistently serialized representation. Exact reproduction compares this representation. A genome encodes it in recoverable numerical or RGB records.

Color tells you what you are reading

Role colors group operations: cyan for input, blue for process, amber for judgment and evidence, violet for quote and reconstruction, coral for action, green for report. Operation labels identify individual operations. The exact RGB genome is a separate encoding of source bytes.

A recorded-value lens reads one declared numeric quantity from a recorded task. Its fixed violet-to-pale-gold scale keeps the same meaning across runs. Values below or above its domain retain their original number and receive an explicit status. Zero is valid; missing, invalid and previous-source measurements remain distinct.

Task parameters belong to the calculation

A graph is a finite collection of operations connected by ordered inputs. A node ID names an operation; its opcode chooses the calculation; its parameters configure that calculation. A source constant differs from an external input supplied for one run. Output IDs select which results the task returns.
